About

Roman Shor, Ph.D., P.Eng., is an Associate Professor and Douglas Von Gonten Faculty Fellow in the Harold Vance Department of Petroleum Engineering at Texas A&M University, where he is also Co-Director of the Geothermal Consortium. Previously, he was an Associate Professor and the Energi Simulation Industrial Research Chair in Geothermal Systems at the University of Calgary, where he remains an Adjunct Associate Professor, and in 2023 he was a Visiting Professor in the Geothermal Energy and Geofluids Group at ETH Zurich. He holds a BA in mathematics and a BSE and MSE in computer science from the University of Pennsylvania and an MSE and PhD in petroleum engineering from the University of Texas at Austin. Before joining academia, he co-founded R5 Automation, a developer of automation systems for drilling rigs. He is interested in energy systems, the interplay of renewable and traditional energy sources, systems thinking, and sustainability.

He led the Wells for the Future Consortium at Texas A&M and his research interests lie in the areas of drillstring dynamics modelling and control, drilling optimization, drilling systems automation and drilling in extreme environments. His team explores topics in deep drilling systems with the goal of reducing well costs to enable deep geothermal systems. He also applies machine learning techniques to problems in the energy sector and is interested in reducing the impact of drilling operations on the environment and surrounding communities. He collaborates with researchers around the world on topics in geothermal energy, drill string dynamics, and the subsurface as an energy asset. He continues his collaboration with researchers in the Energi Simulation Centre for Geothermal Systems Research at the University of Calgary to help plan, develop and monitor geothermal demonstration projects throughout Western Canada.

Current Projects

  • Geothermal energy. Multiple projects investigating drilling for hot and deep geothermal reservoirs, heat recovery from enhanced geothermal systems, systems designs for shallow geo-exchange systems, thermal conductivity and thermal flows in reservoirs, and community engagement.
  • Drillstring modeling. By modeling the drillstring using the wave equation, adding the proper form of distributed friction and investigating the proper couplings between vibration modes, it is possible to develop real time physics based models which may be used for control.
  • Drill bits and hard rock drilling. Bit–rock interaction modeling, rotary percussive drilling in granite, and novel drilling technologies for deep and hot wells.
  • Drilling parameter optimization and vibration reduction through machine learning techniques. By training classifiers using historic datasets which include downhole data, it is possible to identify drilling parameter regions which result in lower drillstring vibration and increased drilling performance.
  • Developing advanced closed loop control for drilling operations. To handle latency and delay in drilling systems, realtime models may be used for feedforward and model predictive control to improve drilling performance.
  • Wellbore stability and integrity. Geomechanics of high-temperature drilling, drilling and casing design optimization, and wellbore isolation for natural gas storage.
  • Machine learning. Applications of machine learning techniques to tool life estimation and fracture optimization. Work ranges from downhole tool life prediction to well cost analysis.

Open Source Projects

Dr. Shor leads the Open Source Drilling Community, part of the SPE Drilling Systems Automation Technical Section (DSATS), which seeks to accelerate drillstring dynamics model development through open source collaboration. As part of this, his team is developing DrillPyze, an open source Python library for simulating coupled axial–torsional drillstring dynamics in real time, including bit models, top-drive controllers, hydraulics and wellbore temperature. You can try the model in the DrillPyze Lite simulator or read the DrillPyze documentation.
